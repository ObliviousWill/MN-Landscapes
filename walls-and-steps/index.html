Walls & steps

Garden walls, steps and retaining

Walls and steps are what turn a sloping garden into a usable one. They are also the elements where cutting corners shows up as a crack.

Retaining ground is a structural job

A wall holding back earth is doing something quite different from a wall standing free. Wet soil pushes hard, and it pushes harder the taller the wall. Retaining structures need a foundation sized for the load, drainage behind them so water is not adding to the pressure, and often reinforcement.

Anything retaining much over a metre needs proper thought, and sometimes an engineer’s input. We will tell you which category your garden falls into at the quote, rather than after the first wet winter.

What we build

  • Free-standing garden walls. Brick, block or rendered and painted, with a coping that throws water clear of the face.
  • Retaining walls and raised terraces. Reinforced where needed, drained behind, and finished to match the house or disappear behind planting.
  • Steps. Set to a consistent rise and a generous going, so people are not counting them on the way up. Wide, shallow flights read far better in a garden than a tight domestic staircase.
  • Edging, plinths and seat walls. The low structures that hold a design together and give you somewhere extra to sit when there are more people than chairs.

Making it match

On a period property the mortar matters as much as the brick — colour, joint profile and how proud it sits all decide whether new work reads as new. We match to the house rather than to the cheapest bag on the merchant’s shelf.

Common questions

How high can I build a garden wall?

Under permitted development a wall is generally limited to two metres, or one metre where it adjoins a road or footpath. Listed buildings and conservation areas have tighter rules. We will flag it if yours needs an application.

Do retaining walls need building regulations approval?

Retaining structures can fall under building regulations and can need structural design, particularly as they get taller or carry a load above. We tell you when that applies rather than build and hope.

Can you match my existing brickwork?

Usually, yes. Bring us a spare brick if you have one; if not we take a sample and match on colour, size and texture, then match the mortar too.

What about drainage behind a retaining wall?

Essential. A drainage layer and a pipe to somewhere sensible is the difference between a wall that lasts and one that bows. It is in every retaining quote we write.
