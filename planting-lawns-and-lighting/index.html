Planting, lawns and garden lighting

The part that turns a built garden into a planted one. It is also the part that decides how the garden looks in five years rather than five weeks.

Planting for the soil you actually have

Norfolk soil varies enormously over a short distance — light and sandy in places, heavy clay in others, chalky further west. It changes what will thrive, how much watering a new border needs in its first summer, and whether the ground needs opening up before anything goes in.

We work out what you have, then choose plants that want to grow in it. It is a great deal cheaper than replacing things that were never going to be happy.

How much gardening do you want to do?

This is the question we ask first, and the honest answer changes the plan completely. A border of structural evergreens, grasses and shrubs looks after itself. A border of herbaceous perennials gives you far more colour and asks for cutting back, dividing and staking. Both are good gardens. Only one of them suits somebody who is out four days a week.

Lawns

  • Ground preparation first. Levelling, breaking up compaction and improving the soil. Turf laid straight onto builders’ subsoil will always look like turf laid onto builders’ subsoil.
  • Turf or seed. Turf gives you a lawn immediately and costs more. Seed is cheaper and better established in the long run, if you can leave it alone for a season.
  • Artificial grass. Genuinely the right answer for some gardens — deep shade, dog runs, roof terraces. It still needs a proper permeable base, and we will talk you out of it if your garden would grow real grass perfectly well.

Lighting, wired in as we build

Low-voltage lighting through planting, along paths and up into trees, on cable run while the ground is already open. Doing it at build stage costs a fraction of retro-fitting it, and it roughly doubles the number of evenings a year you actually use the garden.

Common questions

When is the best time to plant?

Autumn is ideal — the ground is warm, the rain does the watering, and roots establish before spring. Spring works well too. Midsummer planting is possible but commits you to watering.

Will you tell me how to look after it?

Yes. You get the planting plan at handover, so you know what every plant is and what it needs. That is worth keeping — it makes everything afterwards easier.

Do you do regular garden maintenance?

No. We are a design and build company and we would rather do that one thing properly. If you would like a regular gardener afterwards, ask and we will point you towards people locally.

How long before a new border looks full?

Two to three seasons for perennials and grasses; longer for shrubs and trees. We can plant more densely to close the gaps sooner, and thin it later.
