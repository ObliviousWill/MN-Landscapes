Patios & paving

Patios and paving, laid to stay flat

Natural stone, porcelain and block paving, on a sub-base built for the ground it is going into. Most patio problems are not the slab — they are what is underneath it.

What goes wrong, and why

A patio that sinks, rocks or greens over has almost always failed below the surface. Too little sub-base, no compaction, spot-bedded slabs on five dabs of mortar, or nowhere for water to go. None of it shows on the day it is finished, and all of it shows in the second winter.

We dig out to a depth that suits the ground, compact in layers, lay on a full mortar bed, and set falls so water runs off the paving and away from the house rather than sitting on it.

Choosing the material

  • Indian sandstone. Warm, forgiving underfoot and easy to cut into curves. Riven for a traditional look, sawn for crisper joints. Wants sealing if you want to keep it looking new.
  • Porcelain. Dimensionally near-perfect, so joints stay tight and lines stay straight. Barely stains, sheds algae, and comes in plank formats that read like timber. More work to lay, and worth it where you want sharp lines.
  • Block paving. Hard-wearing and simple to lift and relay if services ever need reaching. Best where you want a path or a patio that flows into a driveway.
  • Limestone and granite setts. For edging, banding and detail work that stops a large area of paving reading as a car park.

The details that make it look considered

Where the paving meets the house, the lawn and the borders is what your eye actually reads. We set a proper edge detail, keep cut slabs out of sight lines, and lay out the courses so full slabs land where you look first. It costs a little more in setting out and nothing in materials.

Common questions

What does a new patio cost?

It depends on size, material and what is under the existing ground — which is why we come and look before quoting. The visit is free, the quote is itemised and in writing, and the price we agree is the price you pay unless you ask us to change something.

How long will it take?

A typical patio is one to two weeks on site. Bigger jobs, or ones needing levels changed and retaining added, run longer, and we will give you a realistic finish date with the quote.

Can you lay a patio over the old one?

Occasionally, if the base is sound and the finished height still works against your damp course and door thresholds. More often it is a false economy and we will say so.

Do I need drainage?

Every patio needs somewhere for water to go. On most jobs that means falls away from the house into a border or a soakaway. We design it in rather than bolt it on.
