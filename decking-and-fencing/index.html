Decking & fencing

Decking, fencing and gates

The structures that give a garden its walls and its floor. Both live outdoors permanently, so how they are fixed matters more than what they cost.

Timber or composite

Composite decking does not need oiling, will not splinter, and holds its colour for years — which is why most people now choose it. It costs more up front and it does look like a manufactured product close to, because it is one.

Timber, kept oiled, is warmer underfoot and ages in a way some people much prefer. It asks for a weekend of attention a year. Neither answer is wrong; it depends on whether you would rather spend the money or the Saturdays.

What holds it up

  • A ventilated substructure. Air moving under a deck is what stops it rotting. Joist spacing set for the board, and airflow left at the edges.
  • Posts set properly. Concreted in, with the timber kept clear of standing water. Fence panels come down in a gale because of their posts, not their panels.
  • Falls and fixings. Hidden fixings where the finish deserves them, and a slight fall so water leaves rather than pools.

Fencing and screens

  • Closeboard. The durable, unfussy choice for a boundary, and it takes paint well.
  • Horizontal cedar lath. Contemporary, warm, and it makes a small garden feel wider. Our most-requested screen.
  • Post and rail. For a paddock edge or where you want the view kept.
  • Gates. Made to measure, hung on ironmongery that will still be closing properly in ten years.

Common questions

How high can a fence be?

Permitted development normally allows up to two metres, dropping to one metre where the boundary adjoins a road or footpath. We will tell you if your situation needs an application.

Whose fence is it?

Your deeds decide, and the plan attached to them usually marks the boundaries you are responsible for with a T. Worth checking before anyone takes a panel down.

How long does composite decking last?

Manufacturers typically warrant it for decades against rot and splitting. In practice the substructure and the fixings decide the real lifespan, which is where we spend the care.

Can you build a deck over an existing patio?

Often yes, provided the finished height still works with door thresholds and there is airflow underneath. We will check both before quoting.
